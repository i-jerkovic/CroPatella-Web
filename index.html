CroPatella: patella-based sex estimator for Croatian population

Instructions, method, and performance
  1. Upload a patella STL file (or use one of the examples).
  2. The app automatically computes the centroid size — a rotation- and position-invariant measure of overall size.
  3. A logistic regression on the log of centroid size is applied to estimate sex.
  4. Results appear immediately. No landmark clicking required.

Method

The centroid size is the root-mean-square distance from the geometric centre of the patella to a dense sampling of points on its surface. It is invariant to rotation and translation: the same bone always yields the same value, regardless of how it is oriented in the CT scan.

The deployed model is a logistic regression:

P(male) = 1 / (1 + exp(-(-16.8716 + 5.7939 × log(centroid_size_mm))))

The canonical deployed model was fitted on 300 left patellae (150 female and 150 male) from the seed-42 training partition. The complete analytical sample comprised 383 individuals (192 female and 191 male).

For reproducible deployment, sampling seeds are derived from the uploaded mesh geometry. This preserves the selected 10,000-to-2,048-point feature definition but does not recreate the experiment's specimen-index sampling seeds.

Performance

  • Cross-validation accuracy: 0.848 ± 0.011
  • Held-out left-patella accuracy: 0.858 ± 0.044
  • Held-out sensitivity / specificity: 0.858 ± 0.040 / 0.858 ± 0.063
  • Held-out PPV / NPV: 0.860 ± 0.056 / 0.858 ± 0.039
  • Held-out AUC: 0.927 ± 0.027
  • Contralateral right-patella accuracy: 0.853 ± 0.025
  • Contralateral sensitivity / specificity: 0.858 ± 0.024 / 0.847 ± 0.051
  • Contralateral PPV / NPV: 0.851 ± 0.040 / 0.857 ± 0.019
  • Contralateral AUC: 0.918 ± 0.016
The model was fitted on left patellae and was evaluated separately on contralateral right patellae. Either side may be uploaded, but the side should be recorded with the result.

How to interpret

Centroid size is the root-mean-square distance from sampled surface points to the centroid, in millimetres.

P(male) ≥ 0.5 → classify as male; < 0.5 → classify as female.

Confidence is highest when P is close to 0 or 1, and lowest near 0.5. The training distribution overlaps in the 17–19 mm range, so cases in this band carry more uncertainty.
